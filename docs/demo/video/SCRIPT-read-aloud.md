# BidPilot demo — read-aloud script

Read this **once through out loud** before recording. Natural pace. Slight pauses
at the paragraph breaks. You do not need to sound like an ad — sound like you
mean it.

Target: **about 90–110 seconds**. If you run long, that is fine — we pad the
picture to match your voice.

---

Small firms do the work.

They pave the lots, wire the generators, keep the parks green, move the
students, keep the clinics open. And then an RFP lands — fifty pages of
mandatory this, attach that, show up Tuesday or you never existed — and the
people who could actually deliver the job lose three nights decoding paperwork
they never wrote.

That is not a market failure in the abstract. That is a neighbor who stops
bidding. A good company that quietly decides the State is not for them.

BidPilot started from that feeling. Not from a deck. From watching capable
Rhode Island shops leave money — and dignity — on the table because proposal
departments are a luxury, and buried requirements are a trap.

So we built a grassroots tool with a simple promise: upload the solicitation,
and within forty-eight hours an AI-operated pipeline gives you what a proposal
team would have taken a week to assemble. A clear bid or no-bid. A compliance
matrix with every mandatory line cited back to the page. A first draft grounded
in *your* evidence — not invented praise.

You still decide. You still sign. You still own the submission. The machine
does the midnight archaeology.

This is the live product. Paid packages. Real Gemini agents. An audit trail for
every call — because if AI is going to run the boring parts of a business, it
should do it where you can see it.

BidPilot is a small bet that the firms who keep this place running deserve a
proposal department they can afford — and that the people who do the work
should get to bid on the work.

---

## Recording tips

1. Quiet room. Close other apps. Phone on Do Not Disturb.
2. Sit a forearm from the mic. Speak as if explaining to one person across a
   table — not projecting to a room.
3. If you stumble, pause, then restart that **sentence**. We can leave the
   take as-is or you can re-record the whole pass; one clean take is best.
4. Soft smile in the last paragraph helps the voice without sounding salesy.
